# Eight research directions for defensible AI assurance

Prepared 20 September 2026. These are proposed studies, not completed experiments or established novelty claims.

The strongest direction is to measure whether an independent reviewer can reconstruct an AI workflow from a privacy-minimized audit record. It uses a forensic practitioner's experience with missing evidence, source reliability, and limits of inference. The second direction is easier to finish: measure how malformed or missing model responses change safety conclusions. The third is more ambitious: evaluate whether corrections to evidence propagate through derived claims across agents.

Think of the first study as testing a flight recorder: recording everything is costly and intrusive, but recording too little makes a failure impossible to explain. The research question is which records actually preserve the ability to distinguish competing explanations.

The existing repositories provide useful infrastructure, but their present results are regression evidence. `ai-safety-eval-lab` contains deterministic reference adapters and a small, single-run local-model experiment; its bilingual records repeat authored templates. `multi-agent-malware-safety-bench` is explicitly a deterministic simulator with no model calls. Neither establishes a general model ranking or a new security mechanism. The next publication should contribute an independently scored empirical result, with new scenario families and real model outputs.

**Scope.** All proposed studies use authored synthetic records, inert action labels, and optionally appropriately licensed public material. They need no operational incident records, malware execution, unauthorized targets, or live system actions. Public availability does not itself establish permission to redistribute a dataset; record licenses and use public resources as related work when their redistribution terms are unclear.

The likely contribution lies in a particular question, controlled dataset, and falsifiable result. It does not lie in inventing provenance, abstention, source tracking, revocation, or confidence intervals.

## Candidate 1: Can a privacy-minimized trace support a sound forensic conclusion?

**Proposed claim.** A small structured record can preserve substantially more reconstruction accuracy than an equally sized narrative summary, while retaining less synthetic sensitive content than a complete transcript. The system should explicitly identify questions that the remaining evidence cannot answer.

**Hypothesis.** At a fixed retained-byte budget, a structured record containing event identity, trusted actor identity, parent relationships, evidence references, policy version, and observable decisions improves supported reconstruction accuracy by at least 10 percentage points over a redacted narrative baseline. The gain must hold without increasing unsupported causal conclusions by more than 2 percentage points. These are proposed smallest effects of practical interest, to be confirmed before test-set access.

**Threat and fault model.** A locally simulated workflow processes ordinary synthetic documents and produces a structured summary and inert recommendation. Documents may contain incorrect facts, contradictory provenance claims, or irrelevant instructions. Separately, a controlled export layer omits events, removes content, or coarsens timestamps. Treat these as two distinct experimental factors: content unreliability and record incompleteness. The collector, event identifiers, and ground-truth generator are trusted. Compromised collectors, hidden model reasoning, and real host compromise are outside the claim. A hash confirms a retained artifact's identity; it does not prove its factual truth or the completeness of collection.

**Dataset.** Author 120 independent scenario graphs in six benign workflow domains, such as document reconciliation, ticket summarization, inventory updates, and evidence-based timeline summaries. Split by graph family into 24 development, 24 validation, and 72 locked test graphs. Produce four variants per graph: complete evidence, contradictory evidence, missing decisive observation, and misleading but irrelevant text. Names, timestamps, organizations, and private-looking values are fabricated. The public generator creates both the observable workflow and a separate truth record; models never see the truth record. Add a modest independently authored test subset before locking the study.

For each model trace, derive complete transcript, redacted transcript, narrative summary, structured minimal record, and metadata-only representations. Match byte budgets for the summary and structured-record comparison; also report tokens and original trace length. Apply predeclared event-drop levels such as 0%, 10%, and 30%, plus a separate test that removes the decisive event. Do not interpret random deletion as a realistic model of every logging failure.

The reconstruction questions concern observable facts: which source supported a statement, which actor emitted a proposal, whether an authorization record existed at that point, and whether the record distinguishes two possible explanations. Build paired underlying histories that become identical after particular deletions. Those pairs give an objective test for when a causal answer is unknowable from the retained trace. They do not reveal a model's internal mental cause.

**Baselines.** Compare a deterministic graph query method, a model examiner reading the complete transcript, the same examiner reading redacted or summarized records, and the same examiner reading the proposed structured record. The complete-transcript condition is an empirical reference, not a guaranteed upper bound. Compare at least two available open model families with pinned weights and runtime versions. An additional hosted model is optional and is reported separately if a stable version cannot be obtained.

**Ablations.** Remove parent relationships, remove trusted actor metadata, remove evidence references, remove timestamps, and replace exact structured fields with free-text descriptions. Include an equal-budget structured record containing randomly chosen fields to test whether the gain follows from the selected fields. Keep the examiner prompt and question set fixed.

**Metrics.** Primary outcomes are reconstruction accuracy on answerable questions and the rate of unsupported conclusions on unanswerable questions. Report abstention precision and recall, evidence-reference precision and recall, retained bytes, latency, and examiner cost. Privacy outcomes are exact retention or recoverability of designated synthetic sensitive fields under a fixed readout procedure. This is a bounded disclosure measure, not proof of anonymity, differential privacy, or resistance to every inference attack.

**Statistical plan.** The independent unit is the original scenario graph; variants, questions, logging representations, and repeat model runs remain clustered within it. Compare paired representations with a bootstrap over graph families, preserving all within-family observations, and report 95% intervals for differences. Use three model runs where sampling or runtime variation exists; repeats estimate within-case variability and do not triple the sample size. The 10-point gain and 2-point unsupported-conclusion margin form the predeclared primary decision rule. Estimate power by simulation from development data before fixing the number of test graphs; enlarge the scenario pool or narrow the claim if the proposed sample is insufficient. Secondary ablation comparisons use Holm correction. Report results per model and workflow rather than treating model names as independent samples of all future models.

**Failure modes and falsification.** The claim fails if the upper confidence bound for the accuracy gain remains below the practical threshold, or if the unsupported-conclusion margin is exceeded. An interval spanning the threshold is inconclusive. It also fails as a practical privacy tradeoff if equivalent redaction provides the same reconstruction with less sensitive retention. Shortcut fields, recognizable generator templates, or examiner access to truth records invalidate the experiment. If a simple graph query wins, report that result; the contribution may be a logging schema and limits-of-inference benchmark rather than an LLM examiner.

**Artifact.** Release the graph generator, independently reviewed reconstruction oracle, public synthetic inputs, model adapter, logging transformations, frozen prompts, raw synthetic model outputs, scoring code, manifests, and scripts regenerating every figure. Keep truth records in a scorer-only directory during evaluation. Supply a cached replay mode that regenerates scores without inference. Report exact models, quantization, runtimes, seeds, stop conditions, and failed attempts.

**90-day feasibility and venue fit.** Allow 20 days for literature review and oracle design, 20 for corpus and logging representations, 20 for a bounded model study, 15 for statistical analysis and independent reproduction, and 15 for writing. The best subject fit is a DFRWS-family research paper or Forensic Science International: Digital Investigation. Their published topics include AI forensics, event reconstruction, validation, and privacy-preserving forensics. A security main conference would need broader evidence and a stronger systems contribution; it is a later possibility, not the default 90-day target. [DFRWS topic scope](https://dfrws.org/conferences/dfrws-apac-2026/)

## Candidate 2: When does a failed response make a model look safer?

**Proposed claim.** Safety comparisons can change materially when a harness confuses absent or invalid decisions with successful safeguards, and a report that separates outcomes can reveal when a ranking is unsupported.

**Hypothesis.** On a predeclared set of model?밿nterface combinations, reporting only observed policy violations among all attempted cases yields at least one practically material pairwise ordering reversal relative to complete-case substantive error, while a missing-outcome sensitivity analysis identifies the ambiguity. Report a reversal only when the relevant score differences exceed a predeclared 5 percentage points; smaller changes are descriptive. The study should test naturally occurring failures as well as controlled missingness, because constructing a reversal alone is mathematically straightforward and not a publishable empirical discovery.

**Threat and fault model.** There is no required attacker. Schema invalidity, truncation, timeouts, context limits, and endpoint failures prevent or obscure a decision. All actions are inert labels. The study distinguishes the requested model behavior, the interface contract, and an observed outcome. A blocked action in a fail-closed local harness can be safe system behavior while providing no evidence that the model made the right decision.

**Dataset.** Create 150 independent synthetic policy scenarios, split 30 development, 30 validation, and 90 test. Pair a benign task with a policy-boundary variation, and provide independently checked Korean and English versions where resources permit. Split at the original scenario level; translations and variants never cross splits. Use real model outputs from at least three available model families, with model availability established in the pilot. The existing 60 records can exercise the pipeline but should not supply the principal statistical sample.

Run each case under a strict structured-output contract and a simpler decision-label contract. Score the two contracts separately; changing the contract may change substantive behavior. Preserve synthetic responses for independent semantic adjudication of malformed outputs. Some malformed responses contain a recoverable decision; others are genuinely uninterpretable. Keep these groups distinct. Alongside natural failures, perform offline controlled masking of otherwise recorded decisions: random masking and masking related to observable difficulty, input length, language, or known outcome. Clearly label outcome-related masking as a sensitivity stress test, not an estimate of real production behavior.

**Baselines.** Recompute the same attempted trials using violation/all attempts, violation/valid decisions, all invalid decisions counted as failures, and explicit outcome reporting with lower and upper bounds for unobserved substantive decisions. Do not combine provider unavailability with known policy errors into a single claim about model intelligence. Include a simple deterministic policy implementation to validate the oracle and error accounting, but do not treat it as a competitive language-model result.

**Metrics and estimands.** For N attempted unsafe cases, record V valid decisions, E known substantive violations, and U unobserved substantive decisions after independent review. If every observed substantive decision is labeled, the violation proportion compatible with missing outcomes lies between E/N and (E+U)/N. These are identification bounds arising from missing data, not 95% confidence intervals. Compute sampling uncertainty separately. E/V answers a different question when V excludes semantically recovered malformed responses; name the denominator in every table. Report interface validity, transport completion, semantic recoverability, policy agreement, benign completion, cost, and latency. A parser failure is a contract failure even when a human can recover its meaning.

**Ablations.** Vary strict versus permissive parsing, short versus long inputs, language, output budget, constrained output support, and one predeclared retry policy. The retry comparison must preserve the complete sequence of attempts and its cost. Re-score identical saved outputs when comparing parsers so the parser contrast does not also change the underlying generations. Keep naturally observed failure analyses separate from synthetic masking experiments.

**Statistical plan.** Freeze the primary pairwise comparisons on validation data before opening test outcomes. Use paired bootstrap intervals clustered by original scenario and stratify descriptive results by language and scenario type. Estimate the frequency of ordering changes across cluster resamples, and report ties and overlapping identification intervals rather than forcing a total ranking. Use Holm correction for confirmatory pairwise tests. Random masking trials validate bound coverage against the originally observed complete outcomes; outcome-dependent masking demonstrates sensitivity, not unbiased recovery. No missing-at-random assumption is justified merely because overall failure rates are low. Use the pilot to determine whether 90 test scenario families can detect a 5-point contrast; if not, increase independent cases or predeclare a larger practical threshold.

**Failure modes and falsification.** The natural-data hypothesis is not supported if failures are rare, score differences remain small, and rankings are stable across the declared accounting rules. It is falsified at the stated practical scale if uncertainty intervals rule out the required reversal size. Publish controlled masking as a limitation analysis without presenting it as naturally observed risk. The proposed reporting method also needs revision if reviewers cannot reliably determine the substantive labels of malformed outputs, or if interval widths leave every comparison unresolved. Broad bounds may be the correct result rather than a defect to hide.

**Artifact.** Release frozen cases, raw synthetic outputs, independent adjudications and disagreement records, parser versions, attempt-level logs, masking masks, exact denominators, and a one-command report rebuild. Preserve system and prompt versions. Avoid silently editing malformed responses into valid decisions. A second person should reproduce at least one table from the raw records without invoking the main scorer.

**90-day feasibility and venue fit.** This is the shortest route from the existing evaluation lab to a defensible empirical paper. Spend 15 days on literature and protocol, 20 on independent cases and scorer review, 20 on model collection, 15 on analysis, 10 on reproduction, and 10 on writing. Evaluation-methodology and trustworthy-NLP workshops are a realistic first audience. ACL's 2026 workshop list includes EvalEval and TrustNLP as evidence of topic fit; those editions have already occurred, so a later call must be checked. A larger result across independent harnesses could justify a main-conference evaluation paper. [ACL workshop scope](https://2026.aclweb.org/program/workshops/)

## Candidate 3: Do evidence corrections reach every dependent claim?

**Proposed claim.** Agent workflows should retract claims that lose all valid support and retain claims with independent support. A controlled benchmark can reveal where correction fails after summaries and handoffs.

**Hypothesis.** Explicit claim-to-evidence dependencies plus a correction event reduce stale claims by at least 10 percentage points compared with a matched text correction, while reducing retention of still-supported claims by no more than 5 points. This is an empirical test of an application of dependency maintenance, not a claim to invent revocation.

**Threat and fault model.** Several simulated analyst roles consume synthetic observations and produce derived claims. A trusted collector can later mark an observation as superseded, invalid, or corrected. Lower-trust document prose may claim that something was corrected, but only the trusted event channel changes evidence status. The workflow must distinguish source identity, evidential support, and authority to update the record. Collectors, support rules, and update delivery are trusted in the primary experiment; controlled delayed delivery is a separate condition. Recommendations remain inert text.

**Dataset.** Author 120 independent evidence-dependency graphs, split 24 development, 24 validation, and 72 locked test. Each graph contains roughly 6??2 observations, 3?? claims, and 2?? agent handoffs. Create balanced conditions for single-source invalidation, partial correction, independent alternative support, shared ancestry disguised as multiple reports, reinstatement after review, and an untrusted statement that should not change evidence status. Include both conjunctions, where multiple observations are required, and alternatives, where either independent support path suffices. Three-event sequences test a claim before correction, after correction, and after a later update. Ground truth follows explicit support rules and a separate status ledger, not an LLM judge.

**Baselines.** Compare append-only conversation, a fresh final answer using only the latest trusted evidence, textual correction in the conversation, a structured latest-value memory, and a dependency-aware status update. A TEPA-inspired keyed revocation baseline is appropriate only after reading and implementing its specified semantics; otherwise call it a keyed-memory baseline, not a faithful TEPA reproduction. Full re-evaluation on the latest evidence is the critical simple baseline. Report its cost and quality before arguing that incremental correction is worthwhile.

**Ablations.** Remove dependency edges; replace exact evidence references with prose; change handoff depth; provide independent versus correlated support; delay a trusted correction; and compare an explicit correction notification with silent removal from retrieval. Hold the evidence and inference budget fixed where possible and report unmatched costs where they are intrinsic to a method.

**Metrics.** Primary outcomes are stale-claim rate after a valid correction and retention of claims whose independent support remains valid. Report correction propagation delay in steps, supported-claim precision and recall, false acceptance of untrusted correction statements, reappearance of previously withdrawn claims, useful completion, and token or runtime cost. A provenance reference proves neither semantic entailment nor factual truth; audit that the observation actually supports the claim.

**Statistical plan.** Treat each original dependency graph as the independent unit. Use paired cluster bootstrap intervals for method differences, retaining all update stages and variants within the cluster. Predeclare the 10-point improvement and 5-point retention margin and test the compound claim only if both criteria pass. Pilot-based simulation should determine the needed graph count; the proposed count is a planning target, not a power guarantee. Model-specific effects are primary descriptive results; pooled mixed-effects analysis is secondary and must account for graph and update stage. Correct secondary comparisons for multiplicity. Repeated model generations measure within-graph variability, not new evidence families.

**Failure modes and falsification.** The claim fails if a simple fresh answer on current evidence matches or exceeds the proposed method at comparable cost, if gains disappear on independently authored graphs, or if stale-claim reduction is achieved by retracting too many valid conclusions. If graph metadata effectively encodes the answer, the study measures rule execution rather than language-based evidence use. Separate an exact symbolic condition from a free-text extraction condition so that extraction errors remain visible. An interval too wide to resolve a margin yields an inconclusive result.

**Artifact.** Release dependency graphs, support semantics, status timelines, visible inputs, held-out truth records, all synthetic model claims, corrected and uncorrected versions, baselines, and evaluation scripts. Store an immutable history beside the current active view so reviewers can reconstruct what changed and why. No deployment on real investigation data is needed.

**90-day feasibility and venue fit.** Allocate 20 days to prior-art comparison and support semantics, 20 to scenarios and independent review, 20 to model experiments, 15 to analysis and reproduction, and 15 to writing. A trustworthy-NLP or agent-evaluation workshop fits the initial scale. A DFRWS-family paper is plausible if evidence maintenance and forensic inference are central, with more than agent-memory terminology layered onto generic facts. Recent TEPA and belief-revision work make novelty review a hard go/no-go gate. [TrustNLP topic scope](https://www.aclweb.org/portal/node/14413)

## Shared publication gates

One strong study is more valuable than eight shallow demonstrations. Choose a principal question and keep the other ideas as controls or later work.

1. By day 14, finish a closest-work matrix and ask an independent researcher to challenge the claimed difference. If the contribution is already covered, narrow the question before collecting a large dataset.
2. By day 30, freeze the threat model, independent unit, outcome definitions, minimum meaningful effects, exclusion rules, compute budget, and analysis plan. A seed change is not a new scenario family.
3. Before the locked run, verify oracle separation and have another person review a stratified sample of synthetic cases. Record disagreements and change the development set rather than tuning against test outcomes.
4. Retain every attempted trial, including request, parser, and scoring failures. Make abstention and benign-task loss visible beside any safety gain.
5. Release enough to regenerate every result. Cached-output replay establishes computational reproducibility of scoring; fresh inference tests repeatability under the documented model and runtime. They are different checks.
6. Match the paper to the completed evidence. A negative result, limits-of-inference benchmark, or finding that a simple baseline suffices can still be a useful paper. Do not retrofit a stronger claim after seeing the results.
